Japanese Restaurant Interior Design in Malaysia: Creating an Omakase Dining Experience

GGE Design Studio · Design journal

In an omakase restaurant, the interior is part of the dining experience. Guests sit near the chef, observe each dish being prepared and spend time appreciating the details of the meal. Unlike a conventional restaurant, where the dining room and kitchen are largely separate, this format makes the preparation counter a central feature of the space.

Thoughtful Japanese restaurant interior design helps create that close connection between chef and guest while allowing the restaurant team to work comfortably behind the scenes.

Make the Chef's Counter the Centre of the Design

The counter sets the character of an omakase restaurant. Its shape, position, material and relationship with the rest of the room influence what guests see and how the chef interacts with them throughout the meal.

A straight counter can create a simple, intimate arrangement, while an L-shaped layout may suit a premises with different access points. The design should accommodate the chef's working method, preparation equipment, guest sightlines and comfortable seating. Counter height and depth should be developed alongside the actual equipment and furniture specifications, rather than chosen purely from reference photographs.

Plan for a Calm Arrival and an Intimate Dining Atmosphere

Many omakase restaurants benefit from a gradual transition between the busy surroundings outside and the quieter dining room. A restrained entrance, warm lighting and a simple material palette can establish that change without relying on elaborate decoration.

Consider what guests encounter from the moment they arrive:

  • A clearly identifiable entrance that reflects the dining concept.
  • A reception or waiting area that does not interrupt seated diners.
  • Comfortable counter chairs with appropriate room between guests.
  • A direct view of the chef's work without unnecessary visual distractions.

Privacy matters too. Carefully positioned screens or changes in lighting can distinguish private dining areas from counter seating when the restaurant's floor plan permits.

Choose Materials That Look Better in an Everyday Restaurant

Natural timber, stone, textured walls and muted colours are often associated with Japanese interiors. In an omakase setting, these materials can make the dining room feel warm and understated while keeping attention on the food.

However, finishes must also suit actual restaurant conditions. Counter surfaces must be appropriate for the intended use and cleaning routine. Seating materials, flooring and partitions should be selected with regular maintenance, wear and replacement in mind. Decorative timber can be used in less exposed areas where it is easier to preserve its appearance over time.

GGE's Bashujiayan restaurant project at Intermark KL illustrates a broader design principle: translating a restaurant's culinary identity into a modern interior through considered colours and cultural details. Although the restaurant serves Chinese cuisine, that concept-led approach is equally relevant when developing an authentic Japanese dining experience.

Use Lighting to Highlight the Food and Craftsmanship

Lighting has a direct effect on how guests experience counter dining. Excessively dim surroundings can obscure the appearance of the food, while uniformly bright lighting can take away the intimate character many omakase restaurants aim to create.

Combine controlled ambient lighting with focused illumination at the dining counter. Consider the chef's working light separately so food preparation remains practical. Lighting design should also account for reflections on polished surfaces and the comfort of guests seated close to the counter.

Coordinate the Front and Back of House Before Building

An omakase fit-out requires more than a beautiful counter. Refrigeration, storage, washing facilities, ventilation, electrical services and other technical requirements should be coordinated around the chef's operational needs. Relevant specialists and premises requirements should be considered during design development.

For restaurant interior design in Malaysia, technical decisions are particularly important when renovating an existing shop lot or shopping mall unit. The available services and physical conditions may influence the proposed layout, as they do with other commercial fit-outs.
